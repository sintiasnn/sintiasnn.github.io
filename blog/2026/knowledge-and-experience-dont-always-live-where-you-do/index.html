So I just got back from one of my favorite events of the year. It’s different from the usual tech events I attend — this one is centered around women’s empowerment, specifically in Bali. What I love most about it is the call for speakers — giving Balinese women a chance to share their stories on stage. That’s rare outside of the tech space, and it always brings out the most genuinely interesting talks.

Besides the selected speakers, there’s also a special guest invited directly by the committee. This year, it was a friend of mine — not someone my age, already married, and a founder of a Bali-based footwear brand. She talked about life as a business owner and a Balinese wife and mother — where domestic responsibilities aren’t optional. Her session was great, but it ended before I could ask my question.

Fast forward to the break — I ran into her and finally got to ask.

“Hey, I saw on your Instagram that you went to Jakarta for some kind of brand owner event. How did that go? And who invited you in the first place?”

She told me a fellow owner friend brought her along. And once she got there, she picked up insights about branding that she simply wouldn’t have found staying in Bali. Things like how to build and maintain a brand persona that resonates with a wider audience. One thing that really hit her was this realization: a brand is a reflection of its owner. Which meant she had to start showing up — on social media, publicly — even though she’d always been someone who preferred to work behind the scenes.

She came back from Jakarta with more than just a trip. She came back with a new perspective she could actually put into practice.

That conversation stuck with me. It mirrored something I’d decided for myself not too long ago — step outside of Bali and try speaking at events in other cities — not to get famous, not just to travel, but because I knew that staying in the same ecosystem meant I’d keep meeting people learning the same things, at the same level, asking the same questions. And I’d plateau — quietly, without even realizing it.

One of the most memorable experiences was OpenInfra 2025 in Yogyakarta. My first time going to Jogja alone. My first time getting accepted as a speaker at an IT event outside Bali. The community energy there was something else — different from anything I’d felt in my local circle. There’s something a friend once said to me that I still carry:

we don’t share just to get travel support — we share to invest in long-term learning. Because that’s what the process is. We’re exchanging the threads of experience we’ve built through our work.

And honestly, that’s exactly it.

Every time I stand in front of a new audience, I’m not just sharing — I’m learning. From the questions people ask. From the conversations that open up after the session that I never saw coming.

That said — not everyone has the chance to physically step outside their ecosystem right now, and that’s okay. Because learning doesn’t always come in the shape of a plane ticket or a conference stage.

I didn’t start there either. My first step was being invited by a friend to fill a session at a campus event. Nothing fancy. But something grew from that — a sense that showing up isn’t about how expert you are, it’s about being willing to enter a new room.

From there, I started getting more active in online communities. GDG, for example — they have their own channels where you can mingle, listen in on discussions, and meet people who work in the same space but think about it differently. That counts too. That’s already a way of stepping outside your ecosystem.

So if you’re not quite there yet, start with what’s within reach:

  • Listen to stories from people whose path looks different from yours
  • Join an online community that’s slightly outside your comfort zone
  • Say yes to a friend’s invitation — even if you’re not sure it’s relevant

The bigger things tend to show up after you’ve already started moving on the smaller ones.

Your ecosystem isn’t your enemy. But if you only ever live inside it, you’ll never know what you don’t know. The best knowledge and experiences don’t always wait for you somewhere comfortable. Sometimes they’re in a room you’ve never walked into, a city you’ve never visited, or a quick conversation during a coffee break — like the one I had today. So when the chance comes to step outside your ecosystem — take it.

Thanks for reading this far. I don’t always write about soft skills, but when something sticks with me enough to turn into words — I figure it’s worth sharing. Hope it lands for you the way it did for me.
